# Specific Aims (Draft v3)

## Background

Health AI models are increasingly deployed in clinical settings, yet evaluation remains fragmented, decontextualized, and disconnected from improvement. Models are benchmarked against static datasets using demographic categories (race, sex) that are social constructs, imperfectly collected, and insufficient to capture the structural biases embedded in care delivery. When a model performs equally across Black and White patients, we declare it fair — even as systematic disparities in care patterns (monitoring frequency, intervention timing, documentation practices) create hidden failure modes invisible to standard audits. Meanwhile, the expertise to build, evaluate, and improve health AI remains concentrated in a small number of elite institutions, perpetuating inequity in who innovates and who benefits.

## What Is Dojo?

We propose **Dojo** — a community-powered platform where AI models, autonomous agents, and human experts collectively evaluate, stress-test, and improve health AI systems. Dojo is not an evaluation benchmark. It is a **gym**: a persistent environment where every participant (model, agent, or human) is both tested and coached, where the goal is not ranking but collective improvement.

Dojo operationalizes five principles (LTARC): evaluations are **Local** (contextualized to deployment settings), **Task-specific** (no universal benchmarks), **Agile** (adapting as models and contexts change), **Reflexive** (the platform evaluates itself), and **Community-powered** (governed by its participants, not by any single institution).

## Specific Aims

### Aim 1: Build and validate a modular agent ecosystem for multi-dimensional health AI evaluation

We will develop and validate four flagship evaluation agents: (1) a **Shortcut Detective** that identifies spurious correlations in medical imaging models; (2) a **Care Phenotype Agent** that generates treatment-pattern-based subgroups as alternatives to demographic labels for fairness auditing; (3) an **Environmental Impact Agent** that assesses the full lifecycle environmental cost of AI systems; and (4) a **Value Reflection Agent** that guides teams through structured value articulation before, during, and after model development. Each agent will be independently validated through cross-agent evaluation, community review, and adversarial testing. We will demonstrate that care phenotype-based auditing reveals performance disparities invisible to standard demographic auditing.

### Aim 2: Design and implement a community governance framework grounded in Value Sensitive Design

We will co-design Dojo's governance with diverse stakeholders — including patient advocacy groups, indigenous communities, Global South researchers, clinicians, and AI developers — using Value Sensitive Design methodology. The governance framework will include: (1) a **values charter** that is explicitly designed to be disrupted and reshaped by the community; (2) mechanisms for stakeholder participation that go beyond consultation to genuine co-design; and (3) an incentive structure that rewards sharing, disclosure of failures, and improvement of others' work. We will document and publish the co-design process itself as a model for community-governed AI infrastructure.

### Aim 3: Pilot Dojo with three deployment scenarios and evaluate its impact on model improvement and community capacity

We will pilot Dojo with three use cases spanning industry (commercial clinical decision support evaluation), community (patient advocacy-driven bias auditing), and academic (resource-constrained lab model improvement). For each use case, we will measure: (1) whether Dojo-identified weaknesses lead to measurable model improvement upon re-evaluation; (2) whether the coaching and community support resources enable teams without prior expertise to achieve competitive model quality; and (3) whether community participation shifts participant values and practices (AI plasticity). We will premiere Dojo during an MIT course and onboard external community members, with the explicit goal of the community reshaping the platform's governance and priorities.

## Innovation

Dojo advances the field in three ways. First, it **closes the evaluation-improvement gap**: instead of scoring and leaving, Dojo provides actionable coaching, shared training resources, and community mentorship. Second, it **replaces demographic proxies with care phenotypes**: treatment-pattern-based subgroups that reveal biases hidden by conventional fairness auditing. Third, it **democratizes health AI expertise**: by recording and sharing the entire process (including the chaos), Dojo ensures that the capacity to build, evaluate, and improve health AI is not gatekept by institutional privilege.

## Impact

If successful, Dojo will establish a new paradigm for health AI governance: community-owned infrastructure that makes every participating model, agent, and human more capable. The platform, its agents, its governance framework, and its grant-writing process will all be openly shared — designed to be replicated by any institution, in any country, to ensure equal footing in who gets to innovate in health AI.
